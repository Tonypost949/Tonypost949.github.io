Part OnePlain-English summary — read this page first

My name is Anthony DiMarcello. My mother is Elizabeth Petruccio. We are 43 and 62. We have been homeless since August 4, 2021, when the Sheriff locked us out of our apartment in Irvine during the COVID eviction moratorium, after a judge had already ordered the lockout stopped.

We went into the Huntington Beach Navigation Center because there was nowhere else. It is a tent structure built on an old industrial lot. The County said the site was safe only as long as there was an asphalt cover over the soil. The City built that cover under a one-year warranty, using a crew its own contractor said was not hazmat trained. The soil underneath has hexavalent chromium at 49 times the state's residential screening level — plus lead, arsenic, mercury, and solvents that turn into vapor and come up through the floor.

In November 2023 my mother photographed that cover failing under her bed. Raw dirt on the floor where she slept. She swept it up with a dustpan. The photographs carry the date, the time, and the location inside the file.

In January 2024 she was in intensive care with an infection in her lung and her spine. Chest tube. Intubated. She went from 160 pounds to 116. While she was in that bed, the shelter exited her from the program and told her she could not come back for thirty days. That is written in her own hospital chart, by a social worker, on January 19, 2024 — not by us.

I reported all of it in writing. To the ACLU. To a United States Senator. To the City Attorney. To a federal task force. I have every email and every date.

After that, things happened to me. I was thrown out of the shelter. A criminal complaint was mailed to an address I had left in 2021 — not to where I actually was, which the shelter knew. It became a warrant. I was arrested at a courthouse while helping an elderly woman with her own elder-abuse case. It was the first arrest of my life. In custody I was put on a psychiatric hold, given medication, and I woke up on the floor with a six-staple gash in my head. Then my mother and I turned out to be the only two people from that shelter who were never moved into housing.

I have been hiding — not from the truth, from them. Because every time I have asked for help in writing, something worse has happened to me. I have asked the ACLU, two Senate offices, a congressional office, the City Attorney, and a federal task force. I have no lawyer, no housing, and a mother whose mind is getting worse and who has started to become violent toward me.

What I am asking for

  1. A restraining order, so the retaliation stops.
  2. A written placement determination for both of us — and written confirmation of a specific placement before either of us relocates. My mother will not leave without it, and that is her right.
  3. A protective investigation into the sequence described in this document.
  4. A medical workup for my mother for the exposures on that site.
  5. One named person at one agency who will answer the phone.

What I am not asking for: money, a lawsuit, or agreement with my opinions. I am asking for protection, and for someone with authority to look at the documents.

The only item on that list that produces an order

The first one. That is why the restraining order is the center of this request, and why Part Five is written the way it is.

The consent problem — and why this request is written the way it is

My mother has the right to refuse services. She is an adult, and unless a court has determined otherwise, her refusal is lawful and no agency can override it. Adult Protective Services can assess, investigate, and offer. It cannot compel her to relocate.

I am therefore not asking anyone to move her. I am asking for the thing she says she needs before she will move: a written determination, and written confirmation of a specific placement. If that cannot be provided, I am asking for that refusal in writing, because a written refusal is a document I can act on, and a verbal one is not.

Her unwillingness is itself part of the record. A person who cannot accept help is a person whose ability to protect herself is genuinely in question. That is a fact for a clinician and, if it comes to it, for a court — not an argument for me to make in an email.

Part FiveThe restraining order — how it actually works

A civil harassment restraining order is the one protective order a person can obtain without a lawyer, and it is the only immediate remedy in California law for what is described here.

  1. File Form CH-100 at the Orange County Superior Court, Central Justice Center. The filing fee is waived with Form FW-001.
  2. A judge reviews it the same day or the next court day and may issue a Temporary Restraining Order on Form CH-110, effective immediately, before any hearing.
  3. A hearing is set, usually within a few weeks. Both sides may appear.
  4. If granted, the order can require a person to stay away from me and my mother, stop contacting us, and surrender firearms.

What I must be able to show

A course of conduct that seriously alarms, annoys, or harasses, that serves no legitimate purpose, and that would cause a reasonable person to suffer substantial emotional distress — and, where the conduct is speech, a credible threat of violence.

What I bring

Part Three of this document, the chronology, photographs of any injury, and the retained envelope. Not argument. Documents.

Where I get help filling it out

The Orange County Superior Court Self-Help Center assists with these forms at no cost. 211 can connect me to a legal aid clinic that assists with restraining order paperwork.

Part SixHiding — and how to stop hiding lawfully

I have been concealing my location, my movements, and my communications because I am afraid of retaliation. That is a rational response to a documented pattern. But it has a cost: it isolates me, it makes me harder to help, and it makes me look evasive to the very offices that could protect me. There are lawful ways to be safe that do not require me to disappear.
