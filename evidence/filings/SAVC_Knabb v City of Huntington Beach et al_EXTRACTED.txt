B. Traceability – Groundwater Migration Pathway 
15. The Site sits atop the Orange County Groundwater Basin (Basin 8-1). 
OCWD's Talbert Seawater Intrusion Barrier injects purified recycled water through 36 
wells, creating a persistent inland hydraulic gradient that forces groundwater northeast 
from the Huntington Beach mesa toward Mi dway City. (Exs. 21-22.) The shallow 
aquifer beneath the Site is hydraulically connected to the principal aquifer supplying 
Plaintiff's water provider, the South Midway City Mutual Water Company 
("SMCMWC"), which extracts directly from the downgradient basin. (Exs. 23-24.) 
16. In May 2026, SMCMWC's North Well recorded Cr(VI) at 0.95 μg/L— 47.5 
times California's Public Health Goal of 0.02 μg/L. (Ex. 38.) This detection lies within 
the documented migration pathway from the Site and confirms the plume hypothesis 
in Exhibits 35-A through 35-D filed with the First Amended Complaint. RCRA's "may 
present" standard is satisfied when Cr(VI) appears in a public supply well at 47.5 times 
the state Public Health Goal within a documented migration corridor. Honeywell, 399 
F.3d at 258-62.

C. Airborne Toxic Dust Pathway 
19. The Site's asphalt cap is two inches thick over loose gravel, violating City 
Specification No. 431-92 and Huntington B each Municipal Code § 230.92. (Ex. 20.) 
The City spent $1.2 million on a permanent cap at City Hall in 2008 but only $128,000 
on the Navigation Center's cap. (Ex. Y.) Defendant Mercy House conducts daily high-
pressure washing of the cap, degrading the asphalt and aerosolizing Cr(VI)- and lead-
laced soils; prevailing onshore winds carry this dust northeast toward Plaintiff's 
residence. (Exs. 19, 26.) 
20. Plaintiff has personally observed dust clouds emanating from the Site on 
multiple occasions during periods of high-pressure washing, and has experienced 
respiratory symptoms — including coughing and shortness of breath — on days 
following such washing. These symptoms are documented in Pl aintiff's medical 
records (Ex. 5). The airborne pathway thus presents a concrete, ongoing exposure route 
independent of groundwater migration.

VIII. TIMELINESS AND CONTINUING VIOLATIONS 
A. Legal Standard 
91. RCRA's imminent-and-substantial-endangerment standard is " expansive," 
requiring only that conditions may present a threat to health or the environment. 
Meghrig v. KFC Western, Inc., 516 U.S. 479, 485–86 (1996). The endangerment need 
not be certain, nor must harm have already occurred; it is enough that contamination 
"may present" a risk. Interfaith Community Org. v. Honeywell, 399 F.3d 248, 258–59 
(3d Cir. 2005). Courts apply a "precautionary, forward-looking" standard that focuses 
on risk, not proof of causation. Laidlaw, 528 U.S. at 181–83.
