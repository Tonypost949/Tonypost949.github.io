II. JURISDICTION AND VENUE 
 
5. This Court has subject‑matter jurisdiction over this action under 28 U.S.C. § 1331 
because Plaintiff’s claims arise under the laws of the United States, including the 
Resource Conservation and Recovery Act (“RCRA”), 42 U.S.C. § 6901 et seq., 
and Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, 42 U.S.C. § 2000d et seq.  RCRA’s 
citizen‑suit provision, 42 U.S.C. § 6972(a)(1)(A)– (B), expressly authorizes 
private parties to bring suit in federal district court against any person who is in 
violation of RCRA requirements or who has contributed or is contributing to the 
handling, storage, treatment, transportation, or disposal of solid or hazardous 
waste that may present an imminent and substantial endangerment to health or the 
environment.  This Court has jurisdiction over Plaintiff’s Title VI claim under 28 
U.S.C. § 1331 because Title VI is a federal statute prohibiting discrimination on 
the basis of race, color, or national or igin by programs or activities receiving 
federal financial assistance.

E. Subterranean Infrastructure and Fraud:  Permanent Subterranean  
Construction Contradicting "Temporary" Claims.  
30.  Upon information and belief, in 2020, as part of developing the Huntington 
Beach Navigation Center ("HBNC" or "t he Site"), Defendants designed and 
installed a comprehe nsive, permanent subterranean stormwater detention 
system. This system included the burial of approximately 2,247 cubic feet of 
high-density polyethylene (HDPE) Stormtech storage chambers, along with 
extensive associated PVC piping, constituting a major, permanent physical

This engineering standard is fundamentally inconsistent with a temporary or 
emergency 90-day shelter and demonstrates that from inception, the project was 
intended as a permanent facility.
