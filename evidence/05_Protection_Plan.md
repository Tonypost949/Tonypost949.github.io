## 0. IF EITHER OF YOU IS IN CRISIS RIGHT NOW

- **Medical emergency:** call **911**. Say the address, the person's age, and the main symptom. If she has a PICC line, a spinal history, or trouble breathing, say so first.
- **Mental health crisis:** call or text **988** (Suicide and Crisis Lifeline). Free, 24 hours, and it does not create a police record by itself.
- **Shelter, food, or same-day services:** dial **211** (Orange County). Ask for "housing navigation" and "case management," not just "shelter."
- **You are being threatened or someone is trying to remove you:** do not argue. Ask for the person's name and badge or employee ID, ask what authority they are acting under, and ask for it in writing. Then call counsel or legal aid.

**One rule above all others:** never sign a document in the moment to make a situation stop. Ask for a copy, say "I need to have this reviewed," and leave. Nothing that matters is decided in a hallway.

### 1.2 At every hospital visit
1. Say, out loud and at triage: **"She resided at the Huntington Beach Navigation Center, a shelter built over contaminated soil with a failing floor cap. Please put that in the chart."**
2. Ask the nurse to read back what was entered.
3. Ask for the name of the attending physician and write it down.
4. Ask for a printed after-visit summary before leaving. If they say no, ask for the patient portal address.
5. Photograph the discharge paperwork with your phone.
6. Do not leave without a written discharge plan if she is being discharged to a shelter or the street.

### 1.3 Get the records now, while you are entitled to them
- Request the complete record from each facility in writing. HIPAA gives 30 days (45 C.F.R. § 164.524), one extension permitted.
- Ask for **"the complete designated record set, including social work notes, case management notes, and all scanned documents."**
- Ask for it electronically. Do not accept a summary.

## 2. HOUSING AND SHELTER PROTECTION

### 2.1 If you are in a shelter or applying to one
- Get the guest handbook. Photograph every page. You cannot challenge a rule you cannot quote.
- Put every accommodation request in writing — email or a dated note handed to staff — and ask for a written response within a stated time.
- Keep a daily log: date, time, staff name, what was said, what was done.
- Do not miss a curfew or a sign-up deadline without documenting why, in writing, the same day.
- If you are exited, ask for the reason **in writing** before you leave the property.

### 2.2 The thirty-day bar problem
If either of you is hospitalized again:
1. Notify the shelter **in writing the same day**, from the hospital, with the admission date and the treating physician's name.
2. State that the absence is disability-related and request a reasonable modification of the seven-day rule.
3. Ask the hospital social worker to send the same notice on hospital letterhead. Their letter carries more weight than yours.
4. Keep the sent email. This is the document that converts "you abandoned your bed" into "you requested an accommodation and were denied."

### 2.3 Housing placement
- Ask, in writing, for the **written criteria** used to select residents for supportive housing placement.
- Ask whether you were screened and, if so, for the score and the denial reason.
- If others were placed and you were not, that is a comparison. Comparisons are evidence.

## 3. LEGAL SELF-PROTECTION

### 3.1 The do-not-do list
- [ ] Do not sign a release, waiver, settlement, or "confidentiality" document.
- [ ] Do not accept money, gift cards, or a hotel stay in exchange for silence.
- [ ] Do not speak to an adjuster, investigator, or opposing attorney about the facts. Say: "I have counsel," or "Please put it in writing."
- [ ] Do not delete or edit anything — photos, texts, voicemails, emails, metadata.
- [ ] Do not post about individuals on social media.
- [ ] Do not miss a court date, even if you believe the case is wrong. A missed date creates a warrant; a warrant creates arrests.
- [ ] Do not discuss the case with staff at the shelter or with other residents. It will be repeated.

### 3.2 If police contact you
- Stay calm. Keep your hands visible. Do not run, resist, or argue.
- Ask: **"Am I being detained or am I free to leave?"**
- Say: **"I am invoking my right to remain silent and I want a lawyer."** Then say nothing else about the facts.
- Do not consent to a search. Say: **"I do not consent to a search."**
- Write down the officer's name, badge number, and the time.
- If you are arrested, do not sign anything at the station without counsel. Ask to make a phone call.

### 3.3 If you are recording
California is an all-party consent state for confidential communications (Penal Code § 632). Do not secretly record a conversation. Recording your own treatment by officials in a public place, or recording a conversation you are openly part of with notice, is different — but get advice before doing it. If in doubt, do not record; write it down immediately afterward instead.

### 3.4 Calendar discipline
Put every date in two places — a phone calendar with an alarm and a paper calendar. Include: court dates, claim deadlines, records-request deadlines, medication refills, and every follow-up call you promised to make.

## 4. EVIDENCE PROTECTION

### 4.1 The 3-2-1 rule
- **3** copies of everything important
- **2** different kinds of media (for example, an external drive and cloud storage)
- **1** copy off-site

### 4.2 What must never be lost
| Item | Why |
|---|---|
| Original photographs, unedited | Metadata is the proof. Any edit destroys it. |
| Hospital records, complete | Causation and damages |
| Construction contract and County briefing | The cap condition |
| Shelter handbook and schedules | The policy that caused the harm |
| Every email you sent to an agency | Proves notice and disproves "nobody told us" |
| Warrant envelope and U-Haul correspondence | The retaliation claim |
| Voicemails | Save as audio files, not transcripts only |

### 4.3 Chain of custody, simply
For any original document or photograph: make a copy, write on the **copy** where it came from and the date you obtained it, and store the original untouched. Keep a one-page list: item, source, date obtained, where stored. That list is what you hand to a lawyer.

### 4.4 Litigation hold
Once you have counsel, ask them to send a written litigation hold to the City, the County, Mercy House, Scripps, Hoag, Mission Hills, and the Sheriff-Coroner. That letter legally obligates them to preserve records. It is one page and it prevents the most common defense: "the records were destroyed in the ordinary course."

## 5. DIGITAL AND FINANCIAL PROTECTION

### 5.1 Accounts
- Turn on two-factor authentication on every email account that contains case material.
- Change passwords to unique passphrases. Store them in a password manager, not a note.
- Do not use the same password for the shelter portal and your email.
- Export and archive the case emails now. An account you cannot access is an account you cannot prove.

### 5.2 Identity theft
- File or update a report at **IdentityTheft.gov** and keep the report number.
- Place a free credit freeze with all three bureaus. It is free and it does not hurt your credit.
- Review the ChexSystems and Early Warning reports, not just the credit bureaus.
- Keep the police report numbers already in the record: HBPD #2023-600391 / T23000504; OCSD #23-018358.

### 5.3 Benefits
- Confirm Medi-Cal and CalFresh are active before a hospital bill becomes a collections matter.
- If a bill arrives for care that should be covered, do not pay it and do not ignore it. Call the facility's financial assistance office and ask for a charity care screening in writing.

## 6. PERSONAL SAFETY

### 6.1 Physical
- Keep a small go-bag: medications (seven days), documents, phone charger, water, a change of clothes, and a printed list of contacts.
- Keep the medical binder and the document list in the bag, not on a shelf.
- Tell one person where you are going and when you will be back.
- If you feel unsafe at a facility, tell staff in writing. A written report creates a duty to respond.

### 6.2 Emotional
What you have been through is not a normal amount of stress for one family to carry. Six years of displacement, a parent in an ICU, a criminal case you believe is wrong, and a system that has not answered — that accumulates.

- **988** is free, 24 hours, and available by call or text.
- A shelter or legal aid case manager can usually refer to a trauma-informed therapist at no cost.
- If you are ever thinking of hurting yourself, call 988 or go to an emergency room and say exactly that. You will not be arrested for asking for help.

### 6.3 The long game
The single best protection for both of you is **one stable medical home and one stable address**. Everything else in this folder is designed to get you those two things. Prioritize them over every other task on the list.
