## WHY THIS RECIPIENT, AND NOT ANOTHER

| Reason | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **It is the fastest lawful lever** | APS can conduct a welfare check and an emergency placement assessment without a court order. No other office on the list can do that. |
| **It is the correct statutory fit** | The subject is a dependent adult in a caregiving relationship that has broken down and become violent. That is squarely § 15600 et seq. |
| **It creates the record the restraining order needs** | An APS report number and a written response are exactly the corroboration a judge looks for on Form CH-100. |
| **It does not require you to disclose anything sealed** | Nothing is filed, so nothing is sealed. Nothing in this email references a qui tam. |
| **It cannot be forwarded to the shelter as a complaint about the shelter** | The request is framed around her safety and placement, not around the facility. That lowers the retaliation risk of sending it. |

## WHAT TO DO AFTER SENDING

1. Write the date, the time, and the email address on your copy.
2. If no acknowledgment arrives in **three business days**, call and ask for the report number. Write down the name of the person you spoke to.
3. If no response in **ten business days**, send the same email to the California Attorney General's office and note the non-response.
4. Do not send it twice to the same office. A duplicate looks like pressure; a documented silence looks like a finding.

## WHEN SHE REFUSES — WHAT THAT MEANS, AND WHAT IT CHANGES

**The rule.** An adult is presumed to have capacity. A person with capacity may refuse services, including shelter, medical care, and placement. APS can assess, offer, and investigate. It cannot remove her, and it cannot force a placement. LAW ENFORCEMENT CANNOT EITHER, absent a crime or an emergency.

**So the request changes shape.** You are not asking an agency to move her. You are asking for two documents:

1. **A written placement determination** — what she is eligible for.
2. **A written confirmation of a specific placement** — before she goes anywhere.

If either is refused, ask for the refusal in writing. That single document does more for you than a year of phone calls, because it establishes that you asked, that she was eligible or not, and that the system answered.

**Where her refusal actually helps you.** A person who cannot accept help because of a psychiatric condition is a person whose ability to protect herself is in question. That is the gateway to two things, in order of speed:

| Path | What it requires | Speed | Who starts it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Emergency evaluation (Welf. & Inst. Code § 5150)** | Probable danger to self or others, or grave disability, as a result of mental disorder | Hours | Law enforcement, a designated clinician, or a mobile crisis team |
| **LPS conservatorship (Welf. & Inst. Code § 5350 et seq.)** | Grave disability — unable to provide for food, clothing, or shelter as a result of mental disorder | Months | County conservatorship investigator, with medical records |

**Important:** refusing help is not, by itself, proof of incapacity. Do not say "she refuses, therefore she's incompetent." Say what she did, when, and what she said. Let a clinician draw the conclusion.

## FACEBOOK — PRESERVE IT PROPERLY, AND DO NOT ADD TO IT

You said her unwillingness is documented across Facebook. That is useful, and it is also fragile. Posts get deleted, accounts get restricted, and a screenshot without a URL is close to worthless.

**Do this once, in one sitting:**

1. **Screenshot the whole post**, including the date, the account name, and the URL bar. Not the cropped text — the whole screen.
2. **Copy the permalink.** A post URL, not the page URL.
3. **Write it into a list:** date of post, date captured, URL, what it shows, and what it proves.
4. **Download your Facebook data** (Settings → Your Facebook Information → Download Your Information → select Posts, Comments, Messages). That archive has timestamps the other side cannot dispute.
5. **Ask one other person to view the posts and write a one-line statement** saying they saw them on a given date. A witness turns a screenshot into testimony.
6. **Back it up twice**, in two places, and never edit an original file.

**Two things not to do:**
- **Do not post anything new about her condition, her location, or this matter.** Every new post is a new disclosure you cannot control, and it may be used to argue you published private medical information.
- **Do not tag, check in, or name the place you are sleeping.** That is the single most dangerous thing a person in hiding can do.
