### A.3 The cap condition — the single most important document pair

1. The County's briefing states the site was deemed safe to operate as a shelter **only "as long as an asphalt pavement cover was placed over the soil."** — `navigation_center_003.jpg` **[RECORD]**
2. The City's construction contract with TTS Engineering, Inc. — City Doc ID 20-9041/235642, Laserfiche #5406552, amended to **$2,410,716.24** — warrants grading and asphalt paving for **"a period of one (1) year after CITY's acceptance thereof."** (p. 6, § 9, lines 331–340) — `laserfiche_5406552_TTS_grading_contract_FULL.txt` **[RECORD]**
3. TTS Proposal #20390 describes the work as **2" asphalt paving over native**, 14,600 sq ft at $1.62/sq ft, and states **"CREW INSTALLING AC WILL NOT BE 40-HOUR TRAINED."** (lines 1083, 1196) **[RECORD]**
4. No land use covenant or long-term operations and maintenance plan for the cap appears in the produced documents. — **[TO VERIFY]** (confirm by records request to DTSC and the City)

**Why the pair matters:** one document says the cap is the only barrier between residents and contaminated soil. The other says the barrier was warranted for twelve months by a crew that was not hazmat-trained. If both are accurate, then after late 2021 the only engineered control on the site had no enforceable maintenance obligation — before the 2023–2024 shelter period.
