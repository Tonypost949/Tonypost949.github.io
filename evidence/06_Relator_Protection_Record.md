## 2. THE SEAL RULE — THE ONE THING THAT CAN END THIS TODAY

If position A applies:

**31 U.S.C. § 3730(b)(2)** requires the complaint to be filed in camera, remain under seal for at least 60 days, and not be served on the defendant until the court orders it. § 3730(b)(3) allows extensions. Those extensions are routine and often run for years.

**What a seal breach can cost you:**
- dismissal of the action;
- your relator's share under § 3730(d);
- sanctions against you and, potentially, your counsel;
- a **public disclosure bar** argument under § 3730(e)(4) that you must then defeat by proving you are an "original source" — a fight you do not need;
- your credibility with the DOJ attorney handling the matter, which is the only audience that matters at this stage.

**The disclosure matrix. Memorize it.**

| May know | May not know |
|---|---|
| The U.S. Department of Justice, Civil Division, Fraud Section | Any city, county, or state agency |
| The U.S. Attorney's Office for the district | Any shelter operator or funder |
| Your own attorney | The press, in any form |
| The court | Social media, in any form |
| A person you are legally required to tell, after advice | Your own family members, unless counsel approves |
| | This dossier. It should not be sent anywhere. |

**If you have already disclosed something you should not have:** tell your relator counsel today, in one sentence, before they hear it from someone else. The repair is almost always possible when the disclosure comes from you first and is narrow. It is often not possible when it arrives as a surprise in a defendant's motion.

## 3. THE RETALIATION CLAIM — WHAT § 3730(h) ACTUALLY REQUIRES

**The statute, in plain terms:** 31 U.S.C. § 3730(h)(1) protects any **employee, contractor, or agent** — and, since the 2010 amendments, **"associated others"** — who is discharged, demoted, suspended, threatened, harassed, or otherwise discriminated against **because of lawful acts** done in furtherance of an FCA action **"or other efforts to stop 1 or more violations."**

**Remedies under § 3730(h)(2):** reinstatement with the same seniority, **two times back pay**, interest, compensation for special damages, litigation costs, and attorneys' fees. It is a make-whole statute, not a windfall statute — but the fee-shifting is what makes a lawyer able to take it.

**The four elements you must be able to prove:**

| # | Element | Your evidence | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Protected conduct** — a lawful act in furtherance of an FCA action or other efforts to stop a violation | Dated disclosures to DOJ task force, ACLU, Senate offices, City Attorney; subject lines; sent-mail records; any acknowledgment | Have much of it |
| 2 | **Knowledge** — the retaliator knew about the conduct | Who was told, when, by whom; referral chains; the fact that a disclosure to DOJ reached a local actor | **Gap — fill this** |
| 3 | **Adverse action** — discharge, demotion, suspension, threat, harassment, or discrimination in terms and conditions | Shelter exit after disclosure; thirty-day bar; warrant mailed to a vacated address; arrest; custody; involuntary hold; medication; housing exclusion | Have much of it |
| 4 | **Causation** — the protected conduct was a contributing factor | Temporal proximity; pretext in the stated reason; departure from ordinary procedure; inconsistent treatment compared to others | **Strongest argument — build the timeline** |

**Statute of limitations:** the statute does not state one. Courts commonly apply three years, borrowing from § 3731(b) or state law, but the analysis varies. **Have counsel calculate it. Do not assume.**

**One important nuance in your favor:** "associated others." If your mother was retaliated against because of *your* disclosures — the thirty-day bar, the housing exclusion — that language is the hook. It was added in 2010 specifically to reach people connected to the relator. Have counsel evaluate it.

## 4. WHAT YOU ARE PROTECTED FROM — AND WHAT YOU ARE NOT

**Protected:**
- Retaliation in the terms and conditions of your relationship with the entity — employment, contract, agency, or, depending on the court, a program relationship.
- Threats and harassment intended to stop you.
- Being cut off from a benefit *because* you disclosed.

**Not automatically protected:**
- Being disbelieved.
- Being treated badly for reasons unrelated to the disclosure.
- Being excluded from housing for a documented program reason, if the entity can show the reason was real and applied consistently.
- Criminal exposure that predates and is independent of the disclosure. This is why the warrant matters so much: the question is not "was there a warrant," it is "was the warrant the mechanism."

## 6. THE PROTECTION PROTOCOL

### 6.1 Communications
- Move case communication to **one** channel that is not the shelter's, not a shared device, and not a work account.
- Never put the words "qui tam," "relator," "seal," or "False Claims Act" in a subject line to anyone outside DOJ and your counsel.
- Assume anything you send to a city, county, or nonprofit address will be forwarded to the entity you are complaining about. Sometimes that is the point. Usually it is a mistake.
- Keep a **disclosure log**: date, recipient, exact subject line, attachment names, and the send confirmation. If it is not logged, it is not proven.

### 6.2 Physical and digital safety
- Two-factor authentication on every account that holds case material.
- Full backup, twice, in two locations, offline.
- Original files never edited. Metadata is your proof of date and place.
- A printed one-page contact card: your lawyer, 988, 211, and one person who will answer at 3 a.m.

### 6.3 When someone asks what this is about
Use one sentence, and only one:

> "I've made disclosures to federal officials and I'm not able to discuss the details. I'd ask that anything about it be put in writing."

Then stop. Do not elaborate. Do not explain your theory. Do not name anyone. The sentence is complete.

### 6.4 If you are contacted by an investigator
- Get the name, agency, and phone number. Verify the number independently before calling back.
- Ask whether you are a witness or a subject.
- Say you will speak with counsel present.
- Do not speculate about anyone's motives, ever, in an interview.

| Resource | What they do | How to approach |
|---|---|---|
| **DOJ Civil Division, Fraud Section** | The office that evaluates and may intervene in an FCA matter | Only through relator counsel if a case is sealed |
| **U.S. Attorney's Office, C.D. Cal.** | Handles FCA matters in this district | Same |
| **Taxpayers Against Fraud (TAF)** | Nonprofit that supports FCA relators and connects them with experienced counsel | Public intake |
| **Government Accountability Project (GAP)** | Whistleblower legal and advocacy support | Public intake |
| **National Whistleblower Center** | Referral and advocacy | Public intake |
| **California AG, False Claims Unit** | State FCA matters, Gov. Code § 12650 et seq. | Through counsel |
| **A whistleblower-protection employment lawyer** | The § 3730(h) retaliation claim itself | Contingency or fee-shifting |

**The distinction that matters:** the FCA lawyer protects the *case*. The employment lawyer protects *you*. They are frequently not the same person, and the second one is the one who handles the retaliation.
