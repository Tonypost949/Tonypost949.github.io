# PART ONE — PLAIN ENGLISH SUMMARY

**Read this page. It is the whole thing.**

My name is Anthony DiMarcello. My mother is Elizabeth Petruccio. We are 43 and 62. We are homeless. We have been homeless since August 4, 2021, when the Sheriff locked us out of our apartment in Irvine during the COVID eviction moratorium, after a judge had already ordered the lockout stayed.

We went into the Huntington Beach Navigation Center because we had nowhere else. It is a tent structure built on an old industrial lot. The County said the site was safe **only as long as there was an asphalt cover over the soil**. The City built that cover under a **one-year warranty**, with a crew its own contractor said was **not hazmat trained**. The soil underneath has hexavalent chromium at **49 times** the state's residential screening level, plus lead, arsenic, mercury, and solvents that turn into vapor.

In November 2023 my mother photographed the cover failing under her bed. Raw dirt on the floor where she slept. She swept it up with a dustpan.

In January 2024 she was in intensive care with an infection in her lung and her spine. She had a chest tube. She was intubated. She went from 160 pounds to 116. While she was in that bed, the shelter **exited her from the program** and told her she could not come back for **thirty days**.

I made written reports about all of this. To the ACLU. To a United States Senator. To the City Attorney. To a federal task force. I have every email and every date.

**After that, things happened to me.** I was thrown out of the shelter. A criminal complaint was mailed to an address I had left in 2021 — not to where I was, which the shelter knew. It became a warrant. I was arrested at a courthouse, in the middle of helping an elderly woman with her own elder-abuse case. It was the first arrest of my life. In custody I was put on a psychiatric hold, given medication, and I woke up on the floor with a six-staple gash in my head. Then my mother and I were the only two people from that shelter who were never moved into housing.

**I have been hiding.** Not because I am hiding from the truth. Because I am hiding from them. Because every time I have asked for help in writing, something worse has happened to me. I have asked the ACLU, two Senate offices, a congressional office, the City Attorney, and a federal task force. I have no lawyer. I have no housing. I have a mother whose mind is getting worse and who has started to become violent.

**What I am asking for:**

1. **A restraining order** so the retaliation stops.
2. **A safe place to live** — today, not on a waiting list.
3. **A protective investigation** into the sequence of events described in this document.
4. **A medical workup** for my mother for the exposures on that site.
5. **Someone to read the file and act**, not refer me to another office.

**What I am not asking for:** money, a lawsuit, or for anyone to agree with my opinions. I am asking for protection and for someone with authority to look at the documents.

# PART FIVE — THE RESTRAINING ORDER

A civil harassment restraining order is the one protective order a person can obtain without a lawyer, and it is the only "now" remedy in California law for what I am describing.

**How it works, in plain terms:**

1. I file **Form CH-100** (Request for Civil Harassment Restraining Order) at the Orange County Superior Court, Central Justice Center. Filing fee is waived with **Form FW-001**.
2. A judge reviews it **the same day or the next court day** and may issue a **Temporary Restraining Order** (Form CH-110) that takes effect immediately, before any hearing.
3. A hearing is set, usually within a few weeks, where I appear and the other side may appear.
4. If granted, the order can require a person to stay away from me and my mother, stop contacting us, and surrender firearms.

**What I must be able to show:** a course of conduct that seriously alarms, annoys, or harasses, that serves no legitimate purpose, and that would cause a reasonable person to suffer substantial emotional distress — and, where the conduct is speech, a credible threat of violence.

**What I must bring:** the chronology, the documents in Part Three, photographs of any injury, and the retained envelope. Not argument. Documents.

**What I need help with:** filling out CH-100 accurately. A domestic violence advocate or a self-help center at the courthouse can assist at no cost. **The Orange County Superior Court Self-Help Center** provides this, and **211** can connect me to a legal aid clinic that assists with restraining order paperwork.
