Section 1How to read this document

Every paragraph in this record carries one of four tags. The tags are the point. They let a lawyer, an agency investigator, a judge, or a reporter tell — at a glance — what kind of statement they are reading, and how much weight it can bear.

TagMeaningWeight
RECORDA document exists and says this. The docket number, page, or exhibit is cited.Highest. Documents decide cases.
ACCOUNTOur direct, first-hand experience: what we saw, did, were told, and lived.Strong, and it is ours to give under oath.
ALLEGATIONWhat we contend is true and intend to prove.Normal. Every complaint works this way.
TO VERIFYCame from a secondary source, a memory, or a document not yet confirmed.Must be confirmed before anyone official sees it.

The one rule for using this file

Never send anything tagged TO VERIFY to an agency or a court. Confirm it, then upgrade it. A single unverified sentence in an otherwise strong letter is what an adverse reader will use to discredit the rest — and they will be right to.

RECORD The site at 17631 Cameron Lane contains hexavalent chromium at up to 980 µg/kg — 49 times the DTSC residential screening level of 20 µg/kg and 3.27 times the commercial level of 300 µg/kg — together with elevated lead, arsenic, toxaphene, and 4,4'-DDE. The County's own briefing states the site was approved for shelter occupancy only as long as an asphalt pavement cover was placed over the soil. The City's contract for that cover warranted it for one year and records that the installing crew would not be 40-hour HAZWOPER trained.

RECORD On November 7, 2023, between 12:20 and 12:36 p.m., Elizabeth Petruccio photographed corroded anchor bolts driven through that cap beneath the resident beds, with raw sub-slab sand and soil on the sleeping floor, at GPS 33.706389, -117.988611. One frame shows her sweeping the soil up.

RECORD She was admitted to Scripps Mercy on January 12, 2024 with right pleural empyema, sepsis, and acute hypercapnic respiratory failure requiring intubation, and transferred to a skilled nursing facility until March 14, 2024. Her weight fell from 160 pounds to 116. While she was in intensive care, the shelter exited her from the program and barred her from returning for thirty days.

RECORD Her social workers documented the shelter three times in her chart, including the phrase "SHELTER HISTORY: HB Navigation Center." Across more than twenty-five physician notes and the discharge summary, the shelter appears nowhere. The intake box reads "homeless, unsheltered."

ALLEGATION We contend that the physical condition of the shelter, the failure to maintain the only engineered barrier on the site, and a written policy that penalizes hospitalization combined to cause and worsen the injuries described here. That is our position. It is supported by documents. It has not yet been established by a physician or an exposure scientist, and obtaining that review is the first thing we are asking for.

4.2 The most important document pair in this case

Document one. The County's briefing states the site was deemed safe to operate as a shelter only "as long as an asphalt pavement cover was placed over the soil." RECORD (navigation_center_003.jpg)

Document two. The City's construction contract with TTS Engineering, Inc. — City Doc ID 20-9041/235642, Laserfiche #5406552, amended to $2,410,716.24 — warrants grading and asphalt paving for "a period of one (1) year after CITY's acceptance thereof." RECORD (p. 6, § 9, lines 331–340)

Document three. The contractor's proposal describes the work as 2" asphalt paving over native and states "CREW INSTALLING AC WILL NOT BE 40-HOUR TRAINED." RECORD (Proposal #20390, lines 1083, 1196)

Read together, those three documents say: the cover is the only thing protecting residents from the soil; the cover was warranted for twelve months; and the crew that installed it was not hazmat-trained. If that is accurate, then after late 2021 the only engineered control on the site had no enforceable maintenance obligation — well before the 2023–2024 shelter period.

Why the medical-exit rule is the center of this case

A rule that punishes a hospital stay only operates on people who are hospitalized. My mother was hospitalized for thirty-six days and then twenty-six more. The shelter exited her while she was in an intensive care unit and told her she could not come back for thirty days. That fact is written in her own hospital chart, by a social worker, on January 19, 2024 — not by us, and not after the fact.

  • A federal case exists with the docket entries listed above. RECORD

10.2 What we believe but cannot yet prove to a scientific or legal standard

  • ALLEGATION That the specific dust and vapor from that specific floor caused my mother's specific infection and my specific lung findings. This is our honest belief. It is supported by timing, the photographs, the site chemistry, and six people in one structure with the same class of illness. It has not been established by a physician or an exposure scientist. That is precisely the review requested in Section 3.
  • ALLEGATION That the failures described here resulted from deliberate choices rather than negligence, indifference, or bureaucratic drift. We do not know what was in anyone's mind. We know what the documents say and what the outcomes were.
  • ALLEGATION That the warrant and the arrest were retaliatory. It is our belief. We are asking for an investigation, not for agreement.

10.3 What we are not saying

  • We are not saying any physician intended harm.
  • We are not saying any named individual committed a crime.
  • We are not saying this document proves any claim. It is a record of what exists and what we say happened.

11.2 The causation problem, and the three steps that solve it

ACCOUNT This case does not fail on documents. It fails or succeeds on medical causation — whether a professional will say, in writing, that the exposure was a substantial factor in the diagnoses.

  1. An occupational or environmental medicine physician retained for a records review — not a treating physician — asked for a written opinion on substantial-factor causation.
  2. A certified industrial hygienist to review the Phase II data, the cap design, the breach photographs, and the indoor dust pathway.
  3. Indoor sampling — dust wipes and air samples inside the structure, chain of custody, certified laboratory. Without indoor data the case rests on inference from soil.

Ask every intake attorney specifically whether the firm will advance expert costs. If not, ask for a referral to one that will.

11.3 What not to do

  • Do not sign a release, waiver, or settlement document without counsel.
  • Do not accept a confidential payment or gift card in exchange for silence.
  • Do not speak to an adjuster or opposing attorney about the facts.
  • Do not delete or edit any photograph, email, or message.
  • Do not post about individuals on social media.
  • Do not file a False Claims Act case or a federal complaint pro se.
  • Do not let a deadline pass because a phone call was not returned. Send the letter, keep the receipt, calendar the date.

Section 13Protection plan

13.1 Immediate

  • Medical emergency: 911 — give the address, her age, and the main symptom. If she has a PICC line, spinal history, or breathing difficulty, say that first.
  • Mental health crisis: call or text 988, 24 hours, free.
  • Shelter, food, same-day services: dial 211 (Orange County) and ask for housing navigation and case management.
  • Never sign in the moment to make a situation stop. Ask for a copy and say "I need this reviewed."

13.2 Medical

  • Build one binder and one digital folder: medication list, allergy list (including the clindamycin allergy), one-page diagnosis summary, insurance cards, HIPAA authorizations, advance directive, health care power of attorney.
  • At every visit, say aloud at triage: "She resided at the Huntington Beach Navigation Center, a shelter built over contaminated soil with a failing floor cap. Please put that in the chart." Ask the nurse to read back what was entered.
  • Request the complete designated record set in writing — including social work and case management notes — under 45 C.F.R. § 164.524. Do not accept a summary.

13.3 Housing

  • Photograph every page of the guest handbook. You cannot challenge a rule you cannot quote.
  • Put every accommodation request in writing and ask for a written response by a stated date.
  • If hospitalized again: notify the shelter in writing the same day, state that the absence is disability-related, and ask the hospital social worker to send the same notice on letterhead. That document converts "you abandoned your bed" into "you requested an accommodation and were denied."

13.4 Evidence

  • 3-2-1: three copies, two kinds of media, one off-site.
  • Never edit an original photograph. Metadata is the proof.
  • Keep a one-page custody list: item, source, date obtained, where stored. That list is what you hand a lawyer.
  • Once counsel is retained, ask them to send a written litigation hold to the City, County, Mercy House, Scripps, Hoag, Mission Hills, and the Sheriff-Coroner.

13.5 If police contact you

  • Stay calm. Do not run, resist, or argue.
  • Ask: "Am I being detained or am I free to leave?"
  • Say: "I am invoking my right to remain silent and I want a lawyer." Then say nothing about the facts.
  • Do not consent to a search. Write down the officer's name, badge number, and the time.
