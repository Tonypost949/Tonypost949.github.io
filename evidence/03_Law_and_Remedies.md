## 2. WHAT IS ALREADY IN PLAY

### 2.1 RCRA citizen suit — 42 U.S.C. § 6972(a)(1)(B)
**Against:** Site owners and operators.
**Theory:** The site presents an imminent and substantial endangerment to health or the environment because contaminated soil remains beneath a failed or unmaintained cap at a residential-occupancy structure.
**Status:** Pled in the operative complaint, ECF 115. The State has stipulated on the record that it does not assert the diligent-prosecution bar under § 6972(b)(1)(B) (SAVC ¶¶ 8, 109(b)).
**What must be proven:** that the site may present an imminent and substantial endangerment; that the defendant is a past or present owner/operator; and that the required notice was given.
**What helps most:** indoor air and dust sampling inside the structure, and a maintenance history for the cap.

### 2.2 § 1983 — 42 U.S.C. § 1983
**Against:** The City and County and individual officials in their official capacities, for policies or customs.
**Theory:** Deliberate indifference in the operation of a congregate shelter on a capped contaminated site, and enforcement of movement restrictions, worked a deprivation of liberty and equal protection.
**Key doctrine:** a municipality is liable only for its own policy or custom, not for the acts of employees alone — *Monell v. Dep't of Social Services*, 436 U.S. 658 (1978). The Good Neighbor Policy, the shuttle schedule, the seven-day medical exit rule, and the thirty-day lockout are the strongest "policy" candidates because they are written down.
**Watch:** qualified immunity for individual defendants; Monell's policy requirement; the two-year limitations period.

### 2.3 ADA Title II — 42 U.S.C. §§ 12131–12134; 28 C.F.R. Part 35
**Against:** Public entities — City of Huntington Beach, County of Orange.
**Theory:** The shelter's rules failed to reasonably modify policies for disabled residents (28 C.F.R. § 35.130(b)(7)); the medical-exit rule penalizes disability-related hospitalization; the movement restrictions effectively confine people with mobility impairments; the integration mandate of *Olmstead v. L.C.*, 527 U.S. 581 (1999), favors community-based rather than segregated congregate settings.
**Damages:** In the Ninth Circuit, compensatory damages require proof of intentional discrimination — deliberate indifference — *Duvall v. County of Kitsap*, 260 F.3d 1124 (9th Cir. 2001) (en banc). Injunctive relief and fees do not.
**What helps most:** documented accommodation requests and the agency's written response or silence.

### 2.4 Rehabilitation Act § 504 — 29 U.S.C. § 794; Cal. Gov. Code § 11135
**Against:** Any entity receiving federal or state financial assistance — which includes the shelter operator and the City and County programs that fund it.
**Theory:** Exclusion from participation, denial of benefits, or discrimination on the basis of disability in a federally funded program.
**Advantage over Title II:** reaches private grantees, including Mercy House.

### 2.5 Fair Housing Act — 42 U.S.C. §§ 3601–3619
**Against:** Mercy House, the City, the County.
**Theory:** Discriminatory housing practice and failure to provide reasonable accommodation under § 3604(f)(3)(B), including the failure to accommodate disability-related hospitalization and the exclusion from supportive housing placement.
**Procedural advantage:** HUD administrative complaints are free, do not require a lawyer, and can be filed within one year. HUD investigates. This is one of the fastest routes to an official finding.

### 2.6 California Unruh Civil Rights Act — Civ. Code §§ 51, 52; Bane Act — Civ. Code § 52.1
**Against:** Business establishments, including the shelter operator.
**Theory:** Unruh guarantees full and equal access; a violation of the ADA is itself a violation of Unruh, with statutory minimum damages of $4,000 per offense under § 52. The Bane Act reaches threats, intimidation, or coercion that interferes with constitutional rights — directly relevant to a no-walk-up rule enforced by the threat of immediate expulsion.
**Advantage:** statutory damages and attorney's fees; no administrative exhaustion.

### 2.7 Elder and dependent adult abuse — Welf. & Inst. Code § 15600 et seq.
**Against:** Care custodians and the entities responsible for her care.
**Theory:** neglect and abuse of an elder in a care or custody setting.
**Leverage:** § 15657 permits attorney's fees and punitive damages on clear and convincing proof of recklessness, oppression, fraud, or malice. § 15630 makes reporting mandatory for care custodians — and the failure to report is itself actionable and reportable.
**Note:** whether Ms. Petruccio qualifies as an "elder" (65+) or as a "dependent adult" under § 15610.23 must be analyzed with counsel. Her disability status, not her age, would be the basis.

### 2.8 Premises liability and negligence
**Against:** City, County, Mercy House, TTS Engineering, EEC Environmental, and any successor.
**Theory:** duty, breach, causation, damages. The one-year warranty and the untrained installation crew are the breach evidence.
**Hurdle:** causation requires an expert. See section 6 below.

### 2.9 False Claims Act — 31 U.S.C. § 3729; Gov. Code § 12650 et seq.
**Theory:** federal and state homelessness funds were drawn down for a facility that did not meet the habitability, environmental, or accessibility conditions of the funding.
**Caution:** this is a specialized, high-risk area. It requires a relator with direct knowledge, careful pleading, and experienced counsel. Do not file pro se. If the matter is already under review by a U.S. Attorney's office, the correct step is to preserve and supplement the record, not to file separately.

## 5. WHAT A LAWYER WILL ASK YOU FIRST

Be ready for these five questions. The answers are in `02_The_Record.md`.

1. **"What is your injury and when did you discover it?"** — Date, diagnosis, facility, record page.
2. **"What is the causal link?"** — Not "I believe." A physician or exposure scientist saying the exposure was a substantial factor. This is the single biggest gap in the case.
3. **"Who is the defendant and what did they do?"** — Entity, policy, document.
4. **"What have you already filed, and when?"** — Federal case number, docket entries, agency complaints, claim forms.
5. **"What do you want?"** — Money, housing, an injunction, an apology, a finding. Have one answer.

## 6. THE CAUSATION PROBLEM — AND HOW TO SOLVE IT

This case does not fail on documents. It fails or succeeds on medical causation. Three concrete steps:

1. **Retain an occupational or environmental medicine physician** for a records review. Not a treating physician — a reviewer. Ask for a written opinion on whether the exposure was a substantial factor in the diagnoses.
2. **Retain a certified industrial hygienist** to review the EEC Phase II data, the cap design, the breach photographs, and the indoor dust pathway, and to opine on whether the structure's occupants were exposed above background.
3. **Get the indoor sampling done** — dust wipes and air samples inside the structure, with chain of custody, by a certified laboratory. Without indoor data, the case rests on inference from soil.

Cost is real. Options: contingency fee counsel who will advance costs; a university environmental health department; the county bar's pro bono panel; or a public-interest science clinic. Ask each intake attorney specifically whether they will fund expert review.

## 7. WHAT NOT TO DO

- Do not sign a release, waiver, or settlement document from the City, County, Mercy House, or any insurer without counsel.
- Do not accept a "confidential" payment or a gift card in exchange for silence.
- Do not communicate with opposing counsel or an adjuster about the facts; route everything to counsel.
- Do not delete, edit, or "clean up" any photograph, email, or message. Metadata is evidence.
- Do not post about individuals on social media. Post about documents, if at all.
- Do not file a False Claims Act case or a federal complaint pro se. The pleading standards are unforgiving and a dismissal can be with prejudice.
- Do not let a deadline pass because a phone call was not returned. Send the letter, keep the receipt, and calendar the date.
